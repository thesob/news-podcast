Patricio, the thread running through today is that the AI trade is turning into a geopolitics trade. Nvidia's leadership crisis, the new US chip rule aimed at Southeast Asian data centres, Beijing's promise of "necessary measures" and the Shenzhen trilateral are all one story now. Markets are reacting the way they usually do when the story gets messier: a stronger dollar, falling tech stocks, and emerging-market currencies under pressure (Brazil's real was the biggest loser yesterday). If tonight's Nvidia results or the Shenzhen meeting go badly, I'd watch for that pressure to reach the Chilean peso too. That's a scenario to keep an eye on, not a prediction.
[EN]
For Chile in particular, copper is in an awkward spot. Prices have held up well enough to support export revenue, but the country is producing less (the lowest output since 2003, by the Finance Ministry's numbers), so Chile is missing out on the upside just when "critical minerals" are at the centre of US–China rivalry. The Pax Silica protests in Manila are a reminder that being part of a superpower's minerals strategy can become a domestic political problem. With Kast's Asia tour, ChileWeek China and Washington's minerals push all happening the same week, Chile may soon get more pointed requests from both sides about where its copper and lithium go.
[EN]
Energy is the other common factor. The Iran war, the Houthi advance and mines in Hormuz are pushing fuel costs up, and the effects are showing up as politics on the street: cost-of-living protests in Manila, Chile's truckers' strike over diesel, and Kast at 27% approval. Morgan Stanley's upbeat 2027 forecast for Chile relies on energy costs easing and mining recovering. If the Gulf stays tense, that recovery could slip again.
[EN]
Finally, look at how fragile shared infrastructure looked this week: the Microsoft outage from a bug that sat unnoticed since July, the Optimizely supply-chain attack, AI-powered hacking tools, and banks going down. A few platforms carry a lot of everyday life. For you, the practical takeaway may be modest but real: keep backups and alternatives for the services you depend on, both personally and in any work you do that runs on them.
