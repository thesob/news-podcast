Connecting the Dots.
[EN]
This section is interpretation and informed speculation, not reported news — it's me thinking out loud about patterns across today's stories, not a claim of fact. Treat it as a starting point for your own thinking, distinct from everything sourced above.
[EN]
Patricio, the loudest thread today is that the people with the most authority over AI can’t hold a line for even a week. Dario Amodei helped rally the industry to “pace the frontier,” and within days Trump waved it away with “whoever wins AI, wins.” Layer on the unredacted Microsoft filing calling data-scraping “the largest theft of labor in human history,” and OpenAI marching into law with Astra, and you get an industry simultaneously asking for restraint, defending its land grab, and expanding into new professional turf. That’s not hypocrisy so much as a field moving faster than anyone’s ability to speak for it — which is worth watching, because it means the “frame” for AI is up for grabs rather than settled from the top.
[EN]
The second thread is a world quietly rearranging itself into blocs. Washington is claiming “permanent control” over Greenland, tightening the screws on Russia’s energy buyers, and being deliberately left out of a proposed European security mechanism; Germany votes Sunday with the AfD surging; Norway inches toward Europe; Pakistan and Saudi Arabia knit tighter. The common denominator is hedging — everyone is reducing their dependence on a US that no longer feels like a fixed point. For a small, open, trade-exposed economy, that fragmentation is the real risk: it raises the odds of tariff crossfire and shipping-lane friction that you don’t control and can’t vote on.
[EN]
Which brings it home to Chile. The “copper recession” story matters more to your daily life than anything from Washington: output down 7% through July, Cochilco seeing a full-year decline, and Quiroz putting the hit at roughly a point of GDP right as the economy is already limping toward maybe 1% growth. The one genuine cushion is price — the mining chamber notes copper has run up hard, which can offset thinner volumes and keep fiscal revenue and the peso steadier than the tonnage alone would suggest. But that’s a bet on a single number set in London and Shanghai, and it’s landing in the middle of ChileDay, the capital-markets reform, and a contentious Presupuesto 2027 under a Kast government inclined to cut. If you’ve got exposure to the peso or local rates, this is the quarter I’d watch it.
[EN]
There’s a strange optimism hiding in the AI-energy corner, too. That Guardian piece on “domestic datacentres” — servers heating your hot water while they compute — is a small, concrete example of the compute boom colliding with energy costs and producing something useful rather than just a bigger power bill. Chile, with cheap solar in the north and a serious grid-storage story, is unusually well placed to be on the winning side of “where does all this compute physically live.” Not a confident prediction — more a reminder that the same AI wave straining grids elsewhere could, with the right siting, be an export you’re positioned to sell.
