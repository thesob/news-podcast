Patricio, if there is a single thread running under today's headlines, it is cost — the cost of institutions getting things wrong, and who ends up paying. A 6 billion dollar crypto heist, Microsoft shedding 60,000 people, a train disaster in Spain, delayed cancer diagnoses in Sweden: wildly different stories, but each is really about systems that were trusted to work and did not, and about the bill landing on ordinary people. The US corruption poll is the same instinct expressed at the ballot box. That mood — the machinery is not working for me — is the water the whole news cycle is swimming in right now.
[EN]
For you specifically, sitting in Chile, the most actionable cluster is the economic one, and it is not comforting. Hacienda has just conceded it may be staring at a recession, the promised fiscal adjustment is suddenly fuzzy, and — crucially — the dollar hit a five-month high against the Peruvian sol partly because metals prices fell and the Fed is holding rates. Copper is Chile's lifeblood, and metals down, strong dollar, Fed on hold is close to a worst-case macro recipe for a copper economy: it pressures the peso, nudges up the cost of anything imported or dollar-denominated, and squeezes fiscal room right when growth is weak. If you have savings or income exposure, this is the week to be thinking about currency mix rather than assuming the peso holds.
[EN]
The AFP story sharpens that same point at the household level. The Constitutional Court clearing the megareforma while hundreds of affiliates rush to the courts to block the transfer of their savings is a genuine tug-of-war over who controls your retirement money — and it is happening precisely as the macro picture wobbles. Worth watching how the promulgation lands, because the details of how balances move into the new generational funds could matter a lot for anyone with an AFP.
[EN]
Then there is the energy wildcard. Ships burning in the Strait of Hormuz, in the middle of an Iran–US–Israel conflict, is the kind of thing that can send oil prices up fast — and Chile imports essentially all its oil. Pair that with a weak peso and you have a plausible path to higher pump and heating costs over the coming months. I would call it a low-probability, high-impact risk rather than a forecast, but it is the sort of story that is easy to ignore until it is suddenly in your electricity bill.
[EN]
Finally, the AI layer — Mistral open-sourcing a trillion-parameter model, Anthropic selling prove-the-ROI tooling, Microsoft cutting tens of thousands — is quietly the most consequential long-run shift of all. The center of gravity is moving from can AI do impressive things to is AI actually paying for itself, and who gets displaced while we find out. For a small, open economy like Chile's, the sovereign-AI angle is the one to keep half an eye on: if capable models become genuinely self-hostable and cheap, that is a rare piece of good news for places that do not want to depend entirely on a handful of US platforms.
