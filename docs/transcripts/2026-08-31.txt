Connecting the Dots.

Patricio, the thread I'd pull on first today is sovereignty and the shrinking middle ground between big powers. Iceland just voted to stay out of the European Union's orbit under the shadow of Trump's Arctic ambitions; Canada is bristling over a president who wants to rename its lake; and closer to home, Chile is running military exercises and demanding a formal declaration of respect from Milei's Argentina over Magallanes. These aren't the same story, but they rhyme: small and mid-sized countries are all being forced to renegotiate their footing as the United States pushes harder on its neighbors and the old multilateral anchors feel less certain. For Chile, that's not abstract. The Magallanes friction sits right on top of Antarctic gateway logistics and southern energy and shipping routes, and how Kast handles a nationalist Milei will shape both the border question and the broader Southern Cone alignment.
[EN]
The second thread is the AI capital-and-power buildout, and it runs straight through the copper in Chile's ground. Musk racing to stand up gas turbines, Nvidia extending its lead beyond the GPU, Caterpillar porting mining automation into AI, the United States walling off drones and robotics while China leans on scale — all of it is one enormous bet on compute, and compute is electricity plus metal. Copper is the nervous system of that buildout. If this investment supercycle holds, the demand backdrop for Chilean copper stays structurally strong, which is broadly supportive for the peso and for fiscal revenue. The flip side worth watching: this is exactly the kind of momentum that turns into overbuild, and any wobble in the AI-capex story, or a China slowdown, would hit copper first, so I'd treat copper strength as a tailwind to enjoy but not to count on indefinitely.
[EN]
Third, notice how much of today's news is really about energy costs colliding with climate reality. The Nepal–Tibet flood is a brutal reminder of the physical bill coming due, even as Musk's turbines show the AI boom pulling in the opposite direction on emissions. For Chile, already a serious player in solar, lithium and green hydrogen, that tension is an opportunity as much as a risk: cheap Atacama sun is one of the few places on earth that could credibly power AI-scale compute cleanly. Worth watching whether any of the hyperscaler energy scramble starts looking south.
[EN]
Finally, a smaller but real signal: the isapres offering their cheapest plans in five years and the Arrau pension reform hitting its key days are the kind of domestic pocketbook news that quietly matters more to daily life than the geopolitics. If the capital-markets reform actually eases housing access, that's a genuine local tailwind. None of this is a confident prediction; it's pattern-matching across a single day. But if I had to name one thing to keep half an eye on as a Chilean citizen of the world, it's the copper, AI and peso linkage, because that's where the global story lands most directly in your currency and your cost of living.
