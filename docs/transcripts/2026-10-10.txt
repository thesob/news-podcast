The FEMA-after-the-cuts story out of Florida is the cautionary mirror here: disaster response is a capacity you fund in the boring years or regret in the loud ones.

Third, the copper-and-currency angle, which is really an AI-and-hardware angle. Today's tech stories — memory prices climbing, cheap phones getting squeezed, DDR4 revived, OpenAI's own revenue forecast slipping $20bn — all describe an AI build-out that is voracious for physical inputs even as its financial story gets wobblier. That build-out is metals-hungry, and copper is Chile's paycheck. A sustained data-center boom is structurally good for Codelco and the fisco; a sudden loss of faith in AI economics is the tail risk to watch. The OpenAI miss is the first real crack worth noting, even if it's far from a collapse.

Finally, the justice-and-institutions thread. A Nobel Peace Prize for international law, a US move to sanction the ICC, and a chorus of middle powers — Europe, Canada, Japan, the UN — closing ranks behind the court all landed together. For a country like Chile that has long leaned on multilateral institutions and international law as a small-economy survival strategy, a world where the hegemon is actively kneecapping those institutions is worth watching closely. It's not confident prediction, just a pattern: the rules-based scaffolding is being contested out loud, and smaller states have the most to lose if it buckles.
