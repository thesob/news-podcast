Connecting the Dots.

[EN]
"This section is interpretation and informed speculation, not reported news — it's me thinking out loud about patterns across today's stories, not a claim of fact. Treat it as a starting point for your own thinking, distinct from everything sourced above."

[EN]
Patricio, the thread I keep coming back to today is that the cost of AI is showing up in the physical world. Meta is openly backing away from its 2030 net-zero target because AI data centres are growing faster than anyone planned. TechCrunch's interview argues that data centres, and their pull on power bills and water, became a political issue big enough to feed the midterm blue wave. And COP31 just collapsed without even naming fossil fuels. Put together, it looks like the world is choosing more computing and more energy demand over climate discipline, at least for now. That's worth watching from Chile in two ways. More data centres and grid build-out mean more copper demand, which is good for Chile's main export. But the climate costs keep piling up, and the 35 °C heat forecast for Santiago next week is a small, local reminder of where that trend points.

[EN]
The second thread is nervousness about money. The BBC's San Francisco piece describes a city riding the AI boom while bracing for a crash, and China is cutting margin requirements to prop up its own stock market. When tech stocks wobble and Beijing has to step in, people tend to retreat to safer assets and demand for commodities softens, which usually puts pressure on the peso and on copper prices. Chile's 2027 budget, with spending growth now pushed up to 1.7% by congressional deals, is being written into that uncertainty. If a real AI correction comes, Hacienda's room to manoeuvre could shrink fast. That's a scenario to watch, not a prediction.

[EN]
Third, energy geopolitics is tightening. Iran is promising to use "all its tools" against the US blockade, which is serious enough that its president is turning off his office lights to save power. Ukraine is now hitting Russian ships as far away as Libya and the Gulf of Finland. Both raise the risk premium on oil and shipping. As a country that imports almost all of its fuel, Chile feels that at the pump and in electricity prices with a lag. The Xinhua story on the cherry "air bridge" is a reminder of the other side of the exposure: Chilean farm exports depend heavily on Chinese consumers and on long, fuel-hungry supply chains.

[EN]
Finally, on regional politics, the pattern is consolidation rather than upheaval. Mexico is working more closely with US intelligence, Machado is building a quiet coalition in Venezuela instead of filling the streets, and Spain looks likely to swing right on 29-N. For Chile, a more security-focused, US-aligned hemisphere, plus a possible PP–Vox government in Madrid, could change the tone of trade and migration conversations over the next year. That's not certain, but it's worth keeping on your radar.
