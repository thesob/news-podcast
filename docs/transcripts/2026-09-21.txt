Patricio, the thread I'd pull on first today is oil, because it's the one that reaches Chile most directly. A war that's actually de-escalating and a Brent price sliding back toward a hundred and four dollars is, on balance, good news for a net oil importer like Chile: cheaper crude eventually feeds through to pump prices, transport costs and inflation, which gives the central bank a bit more room. But notice how fragile that relief is. It hangs on a Saudi pipeline that's currently shut and on Houthis still fighting over the Bab el-Mandeb chokepoint. The de-escalation is a Trump talking point and a market hope, not a signed ceasefire, so I'd treat the lower oil price as borrowed calm rather than a floor.

[EN]
The second thread is political realignment, and it's showing up on three continents at once. Sweden's government just flipped on a single seat; the U.S. midterms are being framed as the fed-up versus the well-fed; even Germany's AfD is tempering its own triumphalism. The common denominator is razor-thin margins and anti-establishment energy, which means policy in a lot of places is now hostage to tiny, unstable coalitions. For Chile, itself heading into its own political cycle with figures like Kast in the frame and Bachelet suddenly off the global stage, the lesson is that governing majorities everywhere are thin and volatile, and that makes the external environment of trade, aid and security guarantees less predictable than it looks.

[EN]
Third: the A-I story keeps splitting into two, and the split matters for you as someone in a smaller, open economy. On one side, Washington is deliberately removing guardrails and even the insiders are profiting; on the other, the people closest to the technology, Terry Tao, the creative-commons essayists, the slow-down crowd, are pushing back on where all this speed leads. Chile trying to write its own A-I framework while Argentina sits dead last in adoption is the regional version of that same tension: do you race, or do you set rules? My hunch is that the countries that quietly build judgment and institutions around A-I, rather than either banning it or worshipping it, will look smartest in five years, and Chile has a real opening to be one of them precisely because it's not a frontier player with money on the table.

Finally, the copper-and-competitiveness angle. There's no single copper headline today, but the Peru-accelerating-while-Chile-lags story is the quiet one I'd watch. Chile's fortunes still ride on the metal and on investment confidence, and a neighbor pulling ahead is the kind of slow signal that matters more than any single day's price. None of this is a confident prediction, it's pattern-spotting, but if I had to name one thing to keep half an eye on this week, it's whether that U.N. General Assembly gathering turns Trump's hopefully on Iran into something real. That single word is doing a lot of work in today's oil price.
