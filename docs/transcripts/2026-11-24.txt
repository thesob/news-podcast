Connecting the Dots.

[EN]
This section is interpretation and informed speculation, not reported news — it's me thinking out loud about patterns across today's stories, not a claim of fact. Treat it as a starting point for your own thinking, distinct from everything sourced above.

[EN]
Patricio, the loudest signal today is how much of the world economy is riding on one company's chair. Nvidia's board ousted Jensen Huang, swore it wouldn't take him back, and then reversed itself in under two weeks after the US president leaned in — and markets added roughly a trillion dollars in a day. That tells you the AI build-out is now treated as strategic infrastructure, with the state willing to override corporate governance to keep it running. Put it next to Meta quietly shelving its 2030 net-zero goal and Anthropic automating code sign-off, and the shape is an industry racing for capacity first and asking questions later.

[EN]
For you in Chile, that race is fundamentally a copper-and-power story. Data centers, grids and chips are copper-hungry, so an AI rally that holds is broadly supportive for copper prices and the peso — but today also showed how fragile it is: one scandal knocked Nvidia down, one phone call brought it back. Meanwhile the CEP is floating a partial opening of Codelco's ownership precisely because the state miner needs capital it doesn't have, and the lower house just approved up to roughly US$25.6 billion in new borrowing amid Kast's weakest polling. Worth watching whether a strong copper cycle eases those pressures, or whether volatility in AI sentiment makes Chile's fiscal planning feel like it's tied to Silicon Valley's mood.

[EN]
The second thread is energy and security in a hardening world. Russia–NATO tension is escalating from missiles on Polish soil to talk of a nuclear-armed satellite, Ukraine is striking Russian ships in the Mediterranean, and Europe is locking in Norwegian gas until 2044 while COP31 collapsed over whether fossil fuels could even be named. Chile imports most of its fossil fuels, so a structurally tighter global gas and oil market is a quiet tax on your electricity and transport costs — and the climate-diplomacy breakdown may mean less concessional finance for the kind of renewables and green-hydrogen projects Chile has been courting.

[EN]
Finally, the trade concentration angle: China takes about 87% of Chile's cherries and much of its copper, at the same moment Washington is blacklisting chip intermediaries like Megaspeed and Congress is grilling Huang over China ties. Mexico's deepening intelligence cooperation with Washington shows how Latin American governments are being pulled toward one pole or the other. Not a confident prediction, but the space for a country like Chile to stay comfortably between the US and China looks like it's narrowing — the same dilemma the Körber poll shows Germans wrestling with.
