Connecting the Dots.

"This section is interpretation and informed speculation, not reported news — it's me thinking out loud about patterns across today's stories, not a claim of fact. Treat it as a starting point for your own thinking, distinct from everything sourced above."

Patricio, the thread I keep seeing today is how much of the global AI boom rests on a handful of single points of failure. Nvidia has gone from "the board has full confidence" to "the board is preparing to fire him" in four days, and the market has shed hundreds of billions of dollars on one man's private life. On the same day, TSMC is writing $1bn cheques to its own biggest customers' customers, and CoreWeave is asking clients to prepay because it won't borrow at today's yields. None of this means the AI build-out stops. It does suggest the financing is getting more circular and more fragile, and that sentiment can swing sharply on non-technical news.
[EN]
That matters for Chile more directly than it might seem. The AI capex story is part of the copper bull case: grids, data halls and the gas plants BloombergNEF says are planned at 181GW all need copper. If the AI trade wobbles, copper sentiment and the peso can wobble with it. At the same time, AWS just switched on its Chile region and Kast is pitching it as Chile joining "the big leagues," and the Codelco governance fight (Pacheco versus the government, with a fictitious-production probe in the background) shapes how well Chile can actually capture that demand. It's worth watching whether Codelco's credibility issues become a discount on Chilean copper at exactly the moment the world wants more of it.
[EN]
The second thread is energy and cost-of-living anger. Chile's truckers are striking over a sixth straight fuel increase, Spain's tenants called a general strike that brought out millions, and a Swedish paper mill has stopped because one day of electricity cost 390,000 kronor. Add Israel readying possible strikes on Iran and Tehran saying Hormuz will "never return" to normal, and the oil-price risk feeding Chile's Mepco fuel mechanism stays live. My not-confident guess is that the 74-versus-120-day freeze gap gets bridged, but more fuel shocks would keep testing the Kast government's fiscal room. That collides with an already ugly budget fight in the Cámara.
[EN]
Finally, there's a quieter positive. The UK just grew fastest in two years on AI-driven services, and Duolingo and DeepMind both show AI raising the value of human verification and judgment rather than removing it. For you, as someone watching from Santiago, the practical read might be that the AI dividend accrues to places with services capacity and reliable energy. Chile has the first in part and is fighting over the second, which is worth keeping an eye on as data centres keep arriving.
