Connecting the Dots.

[EN]
This section is interpretation and informed speculation, not reported news — it's me thinking out loud about patterns across today's stories, not a claim of fact. Treat it as a starting point for your own thinking, distinct from everything sourced above.

[EN]
Patricio, today's common thread is fragility at the chokepoints. A router swap at one contractor grounded US aviation. A single Microsoft fault sent hospitals on three continents back to paper. One man's private life knocked the most important company of the AI boom off balance. And an AI app-builder's default settings left passports open to anyone who loaded a page. None of these were malicious. They're what happens when enormous amounts of activity run through very few nodes. I'd watch whether "concentration risk" becomes the political frame for AI and cloud regulation in 2027, alongside the safety frame.

[EN]
For you in Chile, the most direct transmission is copper. Traders are now explicitly pricing copper off AI sentiment ("every time AI takes a hit, copper comes under pressure"), so the metal behind a big share of Chile's exports and fiscal revenue is partly a bet on Nvidia's boardroom. Firm physical demand in China is a cushion, but a weaker copper price usually means a softer peso and a tighter fiscal picture. That lands just as La Moneda has agreed to freeze fuel prices until March 2027. The IEA's oil surplus is the hopeful counterweight. If it holds and Gulf strikes don't escalate, the cost of that freeze could shrink. If Bahrain-style flare-ups spread, it could grow fast.

[EN]
Geopolitically, next week in Shenzhen matters a lot for Chile. Kast will be in the room with Xi on the same trip where Trump, Xi and Putin sit down together, a meeting Kyiv fears could become a spheres-of-influence carve-up. A world that settles into blocs would raise the stakes of Chile's long habit of trading with everyone. Kast's Vietnam and Japan stops look like hedging, and that's worth noticing. Closer to home, Paraguay's bank-and-president collapse is a reminder that "stable, low-tax neighbour" can change in a week. Exposure is concentrated in real estate and a few big Chilean groups.

[EN]
Finally, a quieter pattern: generational economics is becoming the organising grievance across Europe, from French student blockades to SvD calling it the 68ers leaving the bill to their grandchildren, while AI squeezes entry-level work and UBI enters mainstream debate. Chile has its own version of this (pensions, the AFP Fondo E backlash, youth jobs). If it takes hold, it could reshape coalitions in ways today's left/right maps don't capture. That's something to watch, not a prediction.
