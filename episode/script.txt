Connecting the Dots.

"This section is interpretation and informed speculation, not reported news — it's me thinking out loud about patterns across today's stories, not a claim of fact. Treat it as a starting point for your own thinking, distinct from everything sourced above."

[EN]
Patricio, the thread running through today is that the AI boom is starting to be priced like an ordinary industry, with ordinary politics. Anthropic dropping below its IPO price within a week, Nvidia giving back gains, and an S&P correction all suggest investors are separating "AI is transformative" from "every AI asset is worth any price." At the same time, the Nvidia affair shows how tightly the world's most important chip company is now bound to one man and to the White House. When a president talks about pardons and the attorney general won't say whether there's an investigation, corporate governance starts to look like geopolitics. Not a confident prediction, but chip export policy could become more personal and less predictable, and that matters for every economy whose growth story leans on AI demand.

[EN]
That brings it straight home to Chile. Copper is down 19% from its peak and the dollar is back above $1,031, which is a double squeeze: less export income and more expensive imports. Add the Iran war's energy shock, the same force pushing UK bills up 26%, and Chile, which imports most of its fuel, faces imported inflation just as the Banco Central weighs its December decision. If you're thinking about savings, dollar exposure or big purchases priced in dollars, this looks like a period to stay flexible rather than make bold one-way bets. The longer-term copper story (AI data centres, grids, electrification) hasn't gone away; it just looks less like a straight line than it did in January.

[EN]
Regionally, the Brazil–Argentina rupture ahead of next week's Mercosur summit, Colombia's security crisis and Spain's tense election share a common thread: fights over whether elections and institutions are legitimate. For Chile, which is trying to pass its 2027 budget and sort out its own coalition tensions, that's a reminder that cohesion in the neighbourhood is fragile. A divided Mercosur also weakens South America's hand just as Washington readies a new round of tariffs on 16 economies. Worth watching whether Chile is drawn into that list or benefits as a "neutral" supplier.

[EN]
Finally, there's a quieter cultural signal. Tidal deleting AI music, a festive market cancelling bookings over misleading AI images, and a benchmark showing agents fail a third of everyday tasks all point the same way: people are starting to draw lines around what they'll hand to machines. Combined with the heat wave and fire risk at home, the practical lesson might be the old one: build some resilience into your own plans rather than outsourcing all of it.
