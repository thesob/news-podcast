[EN]
The second thread is energy and chokepoints. Mines in Hormuz, attacks on tankers and Kuwait's air defences switching back on all point to more pressure on oil prices and shipping insurance. For Chile, which imports almost all its fuel, that runs straight into the diesel bill. That's exactly where Quiroz just gave truckers a permanent increase in diesel-tax refunds worth 820 million dollars. If oil climbs, the cost of that concession climbs with it, just as Hacienda is trying to protect the drop in the deficit to 1.8 percent of GDP. Sweden's warnings about empty petrol stations before its own fuel-tax rise are a reminder that fuel prices are politically explosive everywhere.

[EN]
Then there's the geopolitics. Trump, Xi and Putin are at one table in Shenzhen, Washington is rebuilding a military alliance in Caracas, and new US rules are cutting Chinese access to American compute. Kast, meanwhile, is in the same city handing out cherries and calling Chile "a serious partner" to China, its largest trading partner by far, with 55 billion dollars in trade through September. Chile's room to manoeuvre may come down to selling copper, lithium and fruit to both blocs without being forced to pick one, while the US gets more assertive in its own hemisphere. That balance looks workable today, but it could narrow quickly if chip-style restrictions spread to other strategic inputs. This is speculation, not a prediction.

[EN]
The net effect on your pocket is mixed. Data centres need a lot of copper, which supports Chile's main export. A Fed that keeps hiking tends to strengthen the dollar and weaken the peso, so imported goods and fuel get more expensive even while copper earnings hold up. I'd watch the peso around the December Fed meeting and how the copper price reacts to Hormuz headlines in the meantime.
