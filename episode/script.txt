At the very same time, copper just hit an all-time record near fourteen and a half thousand dollars a tonne. You are, in effect, watching your country's single biggest export and one of its biggest import costs both surge in the same news cycle. The net effect on Chile depends on which moves more, and right now copper's windfall looks like the stronger of the two forces.
[EN] But here's the paradox worth sitting with: the copper supercycle isn't lifting growth, as more than one analyst put it, and Chile actually trimmed its twenty twenty-six growth forecast even while raising its copper-price view. Record commodity revenue usually strengthens the peso, which would partly cushion those imported oil costs — helpful for you day-to-day. It also hands the Kast government a fiscal windfall at a moment when its approval is scraping a three point four and sixty-two percent of Chileans think it's underperforming. The interesting question is whether that windfall gets spent to buy political breathing room, saved into the stabilization funds, or simply masks the fact that copper money isn't reaching ordinary wages. Worth watching how the Fiestas Patrias mood and the copper story collide over the next couple of weeks.

For the first time, the argument moved markets — European tech stocks hit a six-week low on the mere suggestion of deceleration — rather than staying inside conference halls. For Chile specifically, this matters through two channels: the country has been courting data-center and green-hydrogen investment, both energy- and water-hungry, and a genuine AI pullback would cool that pipeline; and higher-for-longer AI spending has been propping up the same U.S. tech valuations that Chilean pension funds are heavily exposed to. That's the kind of environment where commodity windfalls get politicized fast, and where a government sitting on a copper boom but low trust has a narrow, and probably brief, window to convert money into legitimacy.
[EN] Bottom line for you: I'd keep half an eye on the oil-versus-copper tug-of-war, since it's your currency and your grocery bill, and the other half on whether the AI-slowdown talk hardens into something real, since it's your pension and Chile's investment pipeline.
