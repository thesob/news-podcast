An AI says it solved a Millennium Problem — and mathematics is in uproar. OpenAI announced it cracked the Navier–Stokes existence-and-smoothness problem, one of the seven one-million-dollar Clay Millennium Prize problems, reportedly in about 88 hours of machine work. Within days, 27 Fields Medalists — including Terence Tao, Peter Scholze and 2026 laureate Yu Deng — signed a public "Math and AI" declaration warning that treating famous problems as benchmarks misses the point of the discipline, which is conceptual understanding, not trophy-hunting, and flagging thorny questions of attribution and credit. The Clay Institute has not certified any solution. It's the sharpest sign yet of how fast AI is compressing intellectual authority. Reported by CNN, CNBC, Quanta Magazine, and the mathematicians' own statement.
