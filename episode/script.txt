Connecting the Dots.
[EN]
This section is interpretation and informed speculation, not reported news — it's me thinking out loud about patterns across today's stories, not a claim of fact. Treat it as a starting point for your own thinking, distinct from everything sourced above.
[EN]
Patricio, the thread I keep coming back to today is that AI's acceleration and the energy transition are now pulling against each other in public. On the same weekend a Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis gets machine-verified, Meta says it may give up on its 2030 net-zero goal, one analysis sees Anthropic's compute running mostly on gas, and COP31 can't even agree to name fossil fuels. The scramble for AI capability looks like it's winning the near-term argument over decarbonisation. That isn't a prediction that climate policy is dead, but it's worth watching whether AI demand becomes the excuse that keeps fossil capacity online through the 2030s.
[EN]
For Chile that cuts two ways. AI data centres, grids and the chips inside them are enormously copper-hungry, which supports the long-run case for Chilean copper and the peso. But today's local news is a reminder that this tailwind isn't arriving on its own: the Central Bank says mining output is falling short and investment has disappointed, and TotalEnergies has walked away from H2 Magallanes, the country's flagship green-hydrogen bet. If global climate diplomacy stalls, the premium for "green" Chilean molecules shrinks too, while copper demand from AI may hold up. That could leave Chile more of a commodity play than the clean-energy hub it hoped to be, which is not a confident forecast but a tension worth tracking.
[EN]
The second pattern is how fragile trust has become in digital systems. Within a few days we've seen an FBI crime database apparently hacked, unexplained outages at banks and air traffic control, a study showing AI routers leaking secrets, Signal blocking screenshots against OS-level AI agents, and AI-assisted cheating bringing down a minister in Seoul. The common thread is that the layers between people and their data now include AI agents nobody fully controls. For you that's practical as well as abstract: what you type into AI tools, and which intermediaries carry it, deserves more care than it did a year ago.
[EN]
Finally, the geopolitical temperature is rising at the edges while the big powers talk peace. Drone strikes in the Gulf of Finland, missiles landing in Poland, an assassination plot in Denmark, Iran firing at US radars in Bahrain, all while Washington pushes for trilateral talks before Christmas. Chile is far from all of it, but energy prices, shipping insurance and risk appetite for emerging markets all run through these flashpoints. A messy end to COP31 plus Gulf escalation is the kind of mix that can keep fuel costs in Santiago higher for longer, so it's worth keeping an eye on.
